How to start strength training: your first 4 weeks

By Paul, Paul Welsh Coaching · 3 min read · Last updated: October 2026

Start with two or three full-body sessions a week, built around five basic movements: squat, hinge, push, pull and carry. Pick weights you can control for 8 to 10 reps, finish each set with a couple of reps still in the tank, and add a little at a time. Learn the technique first. The weight comes later.

Before you start

Strength training is for most adults, at most ages. If you have a medical condition, an injury or ongoing pain, are pregnant or have recently given birth, or are unsure whether it is right for you, speak to your doctor first. You can read more in the health disclaimer.

This guide is general, not a personal plan. Use it to understand the shape of a sensible first month, and adjust to where you are.

The five movements that matter

You do not need dozens of exercises. Nearly every good beginner programme is built from five patterns:

  • Squat. Sitting down and standing up under control. A goblet squat or box squat is a friendly place to begin.
  • Hinge. Pushing your hips back with a flat back, as in a Romanian deadlift with light dumbbells.
  • Push. Pressing away from you, such as an incline push-up or a dumbbell press.
  • Pull. Drawing weight towards you, such as a seated row or dumbbell row.
  • Carry. Walking while holding something heavy. A farmer's carry builds grip, posture and trunk strength.

Your first 4 weeks

Two or three sessions a week, 30 to 50 minutes each, with a rest day between. Each session starts with a five to ten minute warm-up, then one exercise for each pattern, for two or three sets of 8 to 10 reps.

  1. Week 1: learn. Use light weights and aim for smooth, repeatable reps. Two sessions is plenty.
  2. Week 2: repeat. Same exercises, two or three sets. Add a third session if you are recovering well.
  3. Week 3: add a little. If every rep felt controlled with a couple to spare, add the smallest increase available, or one more rep.
  4. Week 4: review. Keep the weights steady, notice what feels easier, and pick one thing to improve next month.

How hard should it feel?

A useful rule is to stop each set with two or three reps left in reserve. It should feel like effort, not a fight. If your form breaks down, the weight is too heavy for now. Muscle soreness a day or two after your first sessions is normal. Sharp pain is not, so stop and get advice if you have it.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Doing too much, too soon. Big first weeks lead to big sore weeks and quiet gyms.
  • Changing the programme every session. You need repetition to get better at a movement.
  • Skipping the warm-up. Five minutes of easy movement makes the first working set feel far better.
  • Comparing yourself with the person next to you. They are not on your timeline.

Common questions

How many times a week should a beginner do strength training?

Two or three sessions a week, on non-consecutive days, is a sensible starting point for most beginners. It gives you enough practice to improve and enough time to recover.

Do I need to go to a gym?

A gym makes it easier to progress because you have a range of weights and machines. You can also begin with bodyweight and simple home equipment, then add a gym later.

How long should a beginner strength session take?

Around 30 to 50 minutes, including a warm-up, is enough. Shorter sessions you repeat consistently beat long ones you dread.

Will strength training make me bulky?

Not by accident. Building a lot of muscle takes years of focused training and eating for it. For most beginners the early changes are feeling stronger, moving better and having more confidence.

Beginner calisthenics: where to start with bodyweight training

By Paul, Paul Welsh Coaching · 2 min read · Last updated: October 2026

Calisthenics is training with your own bodyweight. Beginners should start with the basic patterns of push, pull, squat and core, using easier versions such as incline push-ups, rows and box squats. Get comfortable with control first, then make each movement a little harder when you are ready.

What calisthenics actually is

Social media makes it look like handstands and muscle-ups. Underneath, it is simple: you train strength and body control using your own weight, with little or no equipment. Every advanced skill is a series of small, achievable steps, and you start at the first one.

What you need

Not much. A floor, a sturdy table or bench, a step or box to squat to, and a safe place to hang from or row under, such as a gym rig or a bar designed for it. Gymnastic rings and parallettes are useful later, not on day one.

Starter movements and where they lead

Each pattern has an easy entry point and a clear next step. This is why beginners can train alongside experienced people without feeling out of their depth.

  • Push. Incline push-up on a bench or wall, then lower the incline until you reach a full push-up.
  • Pull. Supported hangs and bodyweight rows, then assisted pull-ups. Our first pull-up guide covers this in detail.
  • Squat. Box squat, then a bodyweight squat to full depth, then deeper or single-leg versions.
  • Hinge. Glute bridge, then single-leg bridge and hip hinge variations.
  • Core. Dead bug and plank holds, then harder leverage positions such as hollow holds.

A simple week

Two or three sessions a week is a good start. Warm up for five to ten minutes, then choose one movement from each of push, pull, squat and core. Do two or three sets of a number of reps you can finish with control, with a couple of reps in reserve. Rest a day between sessions.

How to know when to progress

Move on when you can finish every set with clean form and still have a rep or two left. Then pick the next step on the ladder, such as a lower incline or a bit less assistance. If a new version wrecks your form, go back a step. Progress that feels small is still progress.

Calisthenics or weights?

You do not have to choose. Weights are easy to load in small steps, and bodyweight work teaches control and body awareness. Using both gives you a broader base, which is how I coach. See what coaching includes.

Common questions

Is calisthenics good for beginners?

Yes, when you start with scaled versions of the movements. Incline push-ups, supported hangs and box squats let you train at a level you can control.

Can I build muscle with calisthenics?

Yes. Bodyweight exercises can build muscle when they challenge you enough. As you get stronger, you progress to harder versions so the work stays challenging.

Do I need equipment for beginner calisthenics?

Not much. You can start with floor exercises and a bench or sturdy table. Access to a bar or rings helps with pulling movements, and that can be at a gym.

How often should I train calisthenics?

Two or three sessions a week suits most beginners. That is enough to practise and improve, with rest between sessions to recover.
